2. Scope and roles

The operator acts as controller for access, administration, security and service accounting data. Roles for third-party audio and other data uploaded on behalf of an organisation depend on the actual activities: where the operator acts as processor, an Article 28 GDPR processing agreement is required before processing. This notice and the standard service terms do not replace that agreement.

Native app operations performed entirely locally are not automatically sent to this server. Connecting an app sends data needed for requested jobs, its token and the linked account. Import websites, SSO providers and external AI services have their own processing practices to consider before use.

  • Invoicing and mandatory compliance: legal obligations that actually apply (Article 6(1)(c)); the operator must identify them in its retention disclosure.
  • Account protection, abuse prevention, measurement integrity and dispute handling: legitimate interests in service security and legal defence (Article 6(1)(f)), subject to necessity, proportionality and balancing, with objection and review rights.
  • Genuinely optional activities requiring consent: separate, specific and withdrawable consent (Article 6(1)(a)), without conditioning unrelated functions. Signing in or reading this notice does not provide that consent.

Information essential for authentication, execution and accounting is required for those functions; without it the requested service may be unavailable. Social login and unrequested AI features are not required for ordinary editing. Security, approval and job status emails are service messages; usage summaries follow available settings and are not marketing subscriptions.

5. AI processing and human review

Engines process content for generation, stems, lyrics, chords or markers as requested. The standard installation has no general training pipeline using users’ projects; any new purpose requires assessment, a legal ground and advance information. No retention promises are made on behalf of undeclared external providers.

Native app tokens are linked to users and allow usage to be distinguished and aggregated. Verified measurements and estimates have different labels; missing data does not prove zero consumption. Account approval is administrative. Editing features are not designed to make solely automated decisions producing legal or similarly significant effects on individuals. The user may request human review of access, disputes and consumption figures from the operator.

7. Retention, deactivation and erasure

Operational accounts and projects remain available according to the active service and user actions. Logging out or removing a history row does not erase all personal data. Normal account deletion disables access, revokes sessions/tokens and preserves accounting and identity data under the account lifecycle; reactivation may be requested.

Current settings: project grace period {{project_days}} days; expiry of historical accounting sandboxes {{sandbox_days}} days. Ordinary closure temporarily retains projects, recovery archives and accounting. From Deleted user archives, an administrator may instead permanently erase all remaining data with one confirmation, before either deadline: the account, tokens, projects, archive, sandboxes and associated accounting/billing are removed without creating another quarantine. Generated diagnostic bundles expire after 24 hours. Delivered emails, system logs and copies exported by the operator have separate retention.

Username/email reservations, including HMAC digests, prevent reuse across accounts while retained; permanent erasure removes reservations still linked to the account and identifiable reservations from historical accounts: these are pseudonymised data, not automatically anonymous. There is no universal 365-day legal retention period or general duty to retain them forever. The operator must justify and review identity, accounting, log, backup and quarantine retention. Technical settings do not replace a legal ground or automatically extend rights response deadlines.

8. Technical cookies and device storage

  • mta_session: authentication, normally up to 12 hours; logout revokes the session.
  • mta_login_next: internal destination after sign-in or SSO, up to 10 minutes; grants no authorisation and performs no actions.
  • mta_oauth_state: SSO flow protection, up to 10 minutes, only when initiated.
  • mta_social_totp: second-factor verification after SSO, up to 5 minutes, only where needed.
  • localStorage / sessionStorage: editor preferences, autosave, selected models, current project and interface state. Local storage can remain until cleared by the browser or user; session storage ends with the corresponding browser session.

Authentication cookies are technical. This standard application does not integrate third-party advertising tracking or analytics here. The operator must assess added components: non-essential tools are subject to ePrivacy rules and, where required, separate prior consent. Clearing site data in the user’s browser removes sessions or preferences. SSO providers may use their own cookies when the user chooses to visit them.

9. Security

The software provides authentication, MFA, authorisation checks, password hashing, secret protection and diagnostics designed to reduce disclosed data. The operator must properly configure HTTPS, administrative access, updates, backups, SMTP, hosting and external models. No system guarantees zero risk. Breaches require assessment, authority notification and communication to affected individuals in the circumstances and time limits set by law.

10. Rights, requests and complaints

The data subject may contact the privacy address above, even after account closure, and describe the request. Proportionate identity verification may be requested without requiring a new account. Where applicable the data subject may obtain access, correction, erasure, restriction, portability, objection to legitimate-interest processing and withdrawal of consent without retroactive effect. A project archive does not necessarily replace a complete personal data copy or portability of relevant data.

GDPR requests normally receive a response within one month. Justified extensions of up to two further months must be notified within the first month. Requests are normally free, subject to statutory exceptions. A refusal must explain the reasons and remedies. The user may complain to the authority of their habitual residence, workplace or alleged infringement, including the Italian Garante, and seek a judicial remedy.

11. International users and laws

In addition to GDPR and applicable national law, mandatory rights under other laws remain protected when their territorial and organisational requirements are met. Relevant UK processing is subject to UK GDPR and the Data Protection Act 2018 as amended; Swiss processing to FADP; covered California processing to CCPA as amended by CPRA, including knowledge/access, correction, deletion, non-discrimination and, where relevant, sale/sharing opt-out and sensitive-information limits.
